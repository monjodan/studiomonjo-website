We print nothing on these covers. The paper is the first thing you feel.

Each cover is one paper, from one maker. Inside are the same blank pages as in all our notebooks, folded and sewn by hand in Seoul. These are made in Standard size, A5.

Jeonju, Korea · Chunyang P&B

한지Hanji

This cover is hanji, the Korean paper of paper-mulberry bark. It comes from Chunyang P&B, a hanji company in Jeonju. Choi Jangyun opened his paper factory there in 1966; his son, Choi Youngjae, leads it today, and his granddaughter is the third generation.

We found it excellent the moment we held it: thick, with long fibres you can see in the sheet.

  • CoverHanji · 240 g/m²
  • Made byChunyang P&B · Jeonju, Korea
  • PagesNew April Bride, Ultra White · blank · 105 g/m²
  • SizeStandard · A5, 148 × 210 mm

백운지Baegunji

Shanghai, China · CYP

滌煩紙Tea Paper

This cover is Tea Paper, 滌煩紙. It is made in Shanghai by CYP, a maker of fine papers. By the maker’s account, 滌煩 (dí fán), “what washes worries away”, is an old name for tea; half of the fibre comes from used milk cartons and paper cups, and what is left over from tea production is added to the sheet.

We found it in Taipei in November 2026. This colour is called 甘潤, Pine Green.

Shanghai, China · CYP

醇·萃Coffee Paper

This cover is Coffee Paper, 醇·萃 (chún cuì): “rich” and “extract”. It is made in Shanghai by CYP, a maker of fine papers. By the maker’s account, half of the fibre comes from used milk cartons and paper cups, and the sheet contains silverskin, the thin skin a coffee bean sheds when it is roasted.

We found it in Taipei in November 2026. This brown is called 冰搖美式, Americano.

Shizuoka, Japan · Tokushu Tokai Paper

草木紙Somokushi

This cover is Somokushi, 草木紙, literally “paper of grasses and trees”. It is made in Japan, under the name Kami Lab., by Tokushu Tokai Paper, whose speciality papers go back to 1926. Kamiyama Hisateru designed it to show a landscape: mountains, trees, rocks, rivers.

We found it in Taipei in November 2026. This black is called 漆黑, shikkoku: the black of lacquer. Its surface rises and falls.

  • CoverSomokushi 草木紙 · Shikkoku black 漆黑 · 232 g/m²

About this prototype
  • 26 November: this page is now “Unique and Limited editions”, as Jordan named the section. It holds the paper notebooks, made in A5 (Standard) only; Robey is A6 only. The limited editions join them when he has finished them: the line under “Limited editions” is mine.
  • Baegunji is the second hanji he asked for (170 g/m², three colours). Its words come from the paper card written on 26 November; “three generations” is the maker's account. No sheet is photographed, so the three colours are plain swatches and their exact tones are my guess. Whether it is a hanji in the strict sense is not established: the seller calls the family 인견한지 and does not publish the fibres.
  • No photograph exists yet of a notebook with one of these covers. The large pictures are pieces of the sheets: hanji and Somokushi are cut from photographs of the real sheets, Tea and Coffee are the maker's printed colour samples. The small notebooks are renders that use those same pieces.
  • The hanji text is the one Jordan chose on 16 November. The three other texts are the drafts written for the paper cards, with every claim of the maker kept as the maker's account. Two sentences that speak of the card itself were left out.
  • Still open, from the paper checks: the weights of the three sheets bought in Taipei, his own reason for choosing each, whether flecks show, Jeonju or Wanju for the hanji, and whether the makers agree to be named.
  • No price is shown: ₩28,000 and ₩32,000 are both on file and neither is decided. The card is a design; none is printed.
